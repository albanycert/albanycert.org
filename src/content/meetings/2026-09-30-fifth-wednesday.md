NOAA has issued an El Niño Advisory: a very strong El Niño is likely this winter. A strong
El Niño doesn't guarantee a wet winter in Northern California — 1997–98 flooded the Bay
Area, while 2015–16 was just as strong and fell well short of what was expected — but it
raises the odds of big storms, and Albany's winters have shown what one big storm can do to
streets and power. The December 2022 and January 2023 storms are a good reminder: Albany
CERT volunteers were out clearing storm-drain grates so the water could get away, and the
city's own hazard plan credits that with keeping streets open.

We'll spend the first half on what El Niño is and what it's likely to mean for Albany
between now and March, then the practical side: getting your home ready for heavy rain,
flooding, and outages (including how the City's rainy-season sandbag program works —
details are on the city website), and what a block can do together. Then we'll put our
heads together on what's worth doing as a group before the rains come — coordinating supply
purchases like first aid kits for block captains and neighbors, and maybe a hands-on drill
or two.

Whether you're a CERT graduate, a Block Captain, or just a curious Albany neighbor, you're
welcome — you don't need any prior training to attend. Bring your questions about your own
block.
