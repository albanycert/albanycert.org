Albany CERT is providing free disaster preparedness guides to every household in Albany.
The guide offers step-by-step instructions, emergency checklists, and worksheets to help
you and your family prepare for earthquakes, fires, power outages, and other
emergencies. It helps you create a family emergency plan, gather essential supplies,
secure your space against falling objects, practice "Drop, Cover, and Hold On," and
protect important documents.

Guides are available in 16 languages, including English, Chinese, Spanish, Japanese,
Korean, Hindi, Vietnamese, Arabic, Farsi, Russian, Filipino, Armenian, Dari, Hmong,
Punjabi, and Ukrainian. Language-specific guides aren't linked online yet — contact
[AlbanyCERT@gmail.com](mailto:AlbanyCERT@gmail.com) to request one in your language.

This project is supported by the California Governor's Office of Emergency Services
(Cal OES) Listos California program.

## Earthquake

- **Drop** where you are, onto your hands and knees.
- **Cover** your head and neck with one arm and hand; get under a sturdy table if there
  is one nearby, or next to an interior wall away from windows.
- **Hold On** until the shaking stops. If you're under something, hold on with one hand
  and be ready to move with it.
- If you're outside, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, streetlights, and
  power lines before you drop and cover.
- If you're trapped, text or bang on a pipe or wall rather than shouting — shouting can
  fill your lungs with dust.

Source: [ready.gov/earthquakes](https://www.ready.gov/earthquakes).

## Home fire

- Get out low — drop to the floor and crawl under the smoke to your exit.
- Feel a closed door before opening it; if it's hot or you see smoke, use another way
  out.
- Call 911 once you're safely outside, and tell them your location.
- If someone's still inside and you can't safely help them, get out and tell firefighters
  where they are when they arrive — don't go back in.
- If you're trapped, close doors to keep smoke out, block vents with cloth, call 911, and
  signal from a window with a light-colored cloth or a flashlight.

Source: [ready.gov/home-fires](https://www.ready.gov/home-fires).

## Flood / storm

- If told to evacuate, go — and never drive around a barricade; it's there because the
  road ahead isn't safe.
- Don't walk, swim, or drive through flood water — "Turn Around. Don't Drown": even
  shallow, fast-moving water can sweep a person or a car away.
- Stay off bridges over fast-moving water — they can be washed out without warning.
- If you're trapped by rising water, move to the highest level you can reach indoors;
  avoid closed attics, and get onto the roof if you need to signal for help.
- Keep a radio or phone on for official alerts (see below) for current instructions.

Source: [ready.gov/floods](https://www.ready.gov/floods).

## Get official alerts before and during an emergency

- **Albany's own emergency-alert signup** uses Everbridge — see "Sign Up for Emergency
  Alerts" on [readyalbany.org/cert](https://www.readyalbany.org/cert).
- **AC Alert** is Alameda County's mass-notification system for evacuation and disaster
  instructions by phone, text, and email — sign up at the county's AC Alert page
  (linked from [acgov.org/ready](https://www.acgov.org/ready/connect.htm)).
- Both are opt-in — nobody gets these messages automatically.

## After the immediate danger

Once you and your household are safe, this is where the rest of Albany CERT comes in —
checking on your block, coordinating with your Block Captain, and using the Radio Club if
phones are down. See [Block Captains](/block-captains) and [Radio Club](/radio-club).
